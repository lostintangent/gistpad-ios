# Cedar Street refresh

The goal is to make the kitchen and entry easier to use without turning the
house into a construction site for months. We can live with the old cabinet
handles, but the leaking sink and the entry light need attention before
winter. The plan is organized by room, followed by a cost estimate and the
people who can do the work. Estimates are not invoices. A checked item
means we confirmed a decision or finished a task, not that a contractor
has been paid.

## Kitchen

- [x] Measure the sink cabinet
- [ ] Choose a deeper sink
- [ ] Replace the leaking trap
- [ ] Paint the window trim

The sink cabinet is 36 inches wide, which limits the basin we can choose.
I photographed the water stain inside the cabinet and shut off the slow
drip after measuring. The trap replacement should happen before any paint,
so the new trim does not get marked while someone works below it. A deeper
sink would help with large pans, but its final size depends on the cabinet
clearance. I do not want to order a fixture from a photo alone.

For the trim, we can use the same warm white as the dining room. The
test square looks cooler in morning light than it did under the lamp.
I will look at it after sunset as well. If it still feels too blue,
we can choose a warmer shade without changing the schedule. Painting
is a weekend job we can do ourselves, unlike the plumbing work.

## Entry

- [ ] Replace the dim ceiling light
- [ ] Patch the wall by the coat hooks
- [x] Check the door measurements

The entry is narrow, so a low-profile light matters more than a dramatic
fixture. The ceiling box is in the right place, and the electrician can
reuse it if the wiring tests clean. The wall patch can wait until the
light is done. We measured the door twice, including the frame, because
the first measurement only covered the moving panel. That is why the
door task is checked but we have not ordered a new door.

There is a small shoe shelf under the window. It works, although the
top collects keys and mail. I considered replacing it with a bench,
then measured the clearance to the door swing. A bench would make
the entry harder to walk through, so the shelf stays for now. I
would rather solve the mail pile with one tray than buy furniture
that makes the room feel crowded.

## Living room

- [ ] Repair the loose floorboard
- [ ] Move the reading lamp

The floorboard clicks near the south wall when someone crosses the
room. It is not dangerous, but it wakes the dog at night. We can have
the carpenter inspect it while the kitchen work is under way. The
lamp move is simple: the outlet by the sofa is free once the old
extension cable is removed. I want to try the lamp there for a week
before buying anything else.

The rug can stay. We had put it on a shopping list because its color
looked dull in a photo, but in daylight it ties the room together.
Keeping it also gives us a little more room in the budget for the
plumbing surprise we might find under the sink. I will not count a
possible surprise as a confirmed charge, but the contingency line
exists for a reason.

The seven lines total $2,300. That is an estimate, not the amount we
have spent. The contingency is part of the total and should not be
added a second time when someone asks for the budget. The cost of
a new door is not included because we have not decided to replace
it. The sink is the largest single item; the plumbing labor is a
separate quote. If the floorboard needs more than a small repair,
we will revisit the plan before approving the work.

## People and timing

Rowan is the plumber and can visit on November 12. Ellis is the
electrician and has a window on November 14. Noor is the carpenter
who will inspect the living-room floorboard on November 18. Mara
is coordinating the purchases and doing the paint test. None of
these dates is a completed visit. We will confirm them once the
sink is chosen and the light fixture is in stock.

If the sink is not ready for Rowan's date, we should move the
plumbing visit rather than ask for a rushed installation. The
existing sink still works if we keep the cabinet dry, so a few
days' delay is better than fitting the wrong basin. Ellis's work
can proceed independently. Noor's inspection needs clear floor
space, not a decision about the sink.

## Decisions

1. Keep the existing door until we have a reason to replace it.
2. Replace the kitchen trap before painting the trim.
3. Keep the rug and the shoe shelf.
4. Reserve the $300 contingency without treating it as spent.

The first decision avoids a large purchase on incomplete information.
The second sets a dependency between two small tasks. The third is
a reminder that a photograph can make a useful object look worse
than it feels in the room. The fourth keeps the estimate honest.
If an inspection changes one of these decisions, write down the
new reason rather than silently editing the old note.
