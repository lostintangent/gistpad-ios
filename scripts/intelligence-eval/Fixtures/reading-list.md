# Reading ledger

This is the running log I keep before deciding what belongs on my shelf. Volumes in the first
half mean where I am in a series, not how I rate it. I change the current list most often, while
the completed ranking is intentionally slow to move. A series can be excellent without fitting
my mood this week, and I don't want that to reshuffle the finished list.

## Currently reading (volume)

### In progress

1. Harbor Signal (7)
2. Glass Loom (3)
3. Northern Station (12)
4. Paper Observatory (2)

Harbor Signal is what I reach for on a train: short chapters, a noisy cast, and an easy place to
stop when I get off. Glass Loom has a slower mystery, so I save it for evenings. Northern Station
is a long-running comedy with quiet interludes; twelve is only the volume on my bedside table.
Paper Observatory is beautiful but needs my full attention. None of these four has a completed
ranking. I keep their volume numbers here because I regularly forget what to reserve next.

### Waiting on the next volume

- Rain Postcard is waiting for volume 8 to arrive at the library.
- Golden Dial is between English releases; I have finished volume 4.
- Kite Workshop is on hold after volume 2 because its translation is delayed.

These are not in the stopped list. I plan to continue all three when the next volume exists.
That distinction matters when someone asks what I abandoned. A wait for a publisher or the
library is not a judgment about the story.

## What I love in a story

I love the small exchanges in Harbor Signal, especially when a character changes their mind
without a speech explaining it. More generally, I return to stories in which a decision has
consequences two chapters later and the art gives a conversation room to breathe. The panels
can be dense, but I want enough stillness to notice a gesture. A flashy premise alone won't keep
me reading. A strong ending can raise a finished series above something that was fun every week
but never found a destination.

That is why I usually finish before ranking. I write brief reactions while reading, then wait a
week and ask whether the images or a character's choice remain with me. Humor helps too, but I
don't need every chapter to be cheerful. I prefer a series that gives side characters an interior
life and lets the central conflict change shape instead of repeating one fight. I can like a
currently-reading series a lot without claiming that it has earned a top place yet.

## Started but stopped

1. Cloud Cartographer (4)
2. Tin Compass (2)
3. River Engine (6)

I stopped Cloud Cartographer after volume 4. The premise was wonderful, but every new setting
reset the relationships I cared about. I set Tin Compass aside after volume 2 because the
dialogue turned into explanations of its rules rather than people talking. River Engine lasted
six volumes, longer than the other two, and then repeated the same tournament structure.
Stopping at six doesn't mean I liked it more than a finished book. These numbers record the
last volumes I tried, not how I rank the titles.

## Reading notes from this month

### October 3

Harbor Signal volume 7 uses a station clock as a recurring image. The comedy is still quick,
but the scene with the missing timetable slows down and lets an old friendship feel awkward.
I underlined the exchange without adding it to my completed list. The story is ongoing, and a
good volume doesn't tell me how it will end. Glass Loom volume 3 is quieter and has a chapter
almost entirely in blue pencil. It made me go back to the first volume and look for the seam
between the two timelines. I would like to see that thread resolved before ranking it.

### October 9

I borrowed Northern Station volume 12 because the library had it, not because I had decided
it belonged above anything in the finished ranking. The chapter about the empty restaurant
is my favorite chapter this month. Its characters argue over a trivial bill and accidentally
say what they've been avoiding for years. I expect the next volume to change how that scene
lands. Paper Observatory volume 2 introduces a small glossary, which helps with names but
interrupts the pacing. I'll try volume 3 before deciding whether it belongs in the waiting or
stopped section.

### October 15

The last Rain Postcard volume ended on an unresolved journey. The library's copy of volume 8
is still checked out. I don't want to mark it stopped just because there's a queue. I looked at
the art in Golden Dial again and noticed how the backgrounds get emptier when a person is
isolated. That visual idea is more persuasive than its big reveal. Kite Workshop has the
opposite strength: its backgrounds are busy, but its conversations make each workspace feel
inhabited. A story about craft needs to show both the work and the people learning it.

### October 21

I flipped through Cloud Cartographer again to check the last volume I read. Four is right.
The map sketches are still inventive, and the trip through the flooded archive is worth
remembering, even though I don't intend to buy volume 5. Tin Compass stops at two; I returned
both copies. River Engine stops at six, with the tournament unfinished. I keep these notes so
I don't accidentally answer "what did I stop?" with a series merely waiting on translation.

## Completed (ranked)

The numbered lists below are my ordering within each tier. I start each tier at one, so the
number needs its heading. "Exceptional" is above "Really good", which is above "Mixed". A
volume number in the earlier sections is not a competing rank.

### Exceptional (stayed with me)

1. The Cedar Compass
2. Night Orchard
3. A Quiet Harbor
4. Lantern Makers

The Cedar Compass stays first. Its final arc changes the meaning of an early promise without
undoing what the characters learned along the way. I can picture the silent page before the
last decision months after reading it. Night Orchard is second because its art shifts with the
seasons and makes a familiar place feel changed; it is close, but its ending is less precise.
A Quiet Harbor is third: wonderful patience and a beautifully handled side story, though its
middle wanders. Lantern Makers is fourth, with a warm cast and an ending I admire more each
time I revisit it. The ranking is about completed work, not which title I mention most often.

### Really good

1. Orchard Tram
2. The Second Workshop
3. A Map of Rain
4. Clocktower Letters

Orchard Tram has a wonderful small cast and a pace that makes a commute feel like a whole
country. I finished it in one weekend, then reread its middle three volumes more slowly.
The Second Workshop is ambitious and sometimes too crowded, but the scenes at the kitchen
table are unusually honest. A Map of Rain spends more time on scenery than I expected and
turns that into a strength. Clocktower Letters is funny and observant, though its final
reveal answers a question I was happy leaving open. All four are below every Exceptional
entry, even though I reread some scenes more often.

### Mixed

1. Distant Mornings
2. The Blue Thread
3. Iron Garden

Distant Mornings starts with a sharp idea and gets distracted by a side plot. The Blue Thread
has panels I want to frame, but its main relationship stays at the same temperature. Iron
Garden has an exciting first book and a rushed finish. I don't regret reading any of them,
and "Mixed" isn't the same as stopped. They are completed titles, in that order, below
everything in the two higher tiers.

## End-of-month note

I plan to reread The Cedar Compass in December, starting with the opening chapter rather
than the ending. I also want to borrow Night Orchard for a friend and talk about why the
second-ranked book nearly caught up with the first. I have not set a purchase budget for
next year; these notes are about reading, not a spending plan. The next update should check
whether Paper Observatory remains in progress after volume 3. If it does not, write down the
actual stopping point instead of treating its current volume as an opinion.
